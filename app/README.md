# DirectorLink app (PWA)

The DirectorLink app on **https://app.directorlink.io**. It is framework-free HTML, CSS and JavaScript and needs no build step. The API console is its own site (`../console/`, https://console.directorlink.io).

## Structure

- `index.html` — app shell: tab bar, the screen container and `theme-boot.js`
- `theme-boot.js` — blocking script in `<head>`: applies the saved palette, theme and text direction before the first paint (the CSP forbids inline scripts)
- `app.js` — entry module: hash router, renderer, dialogs, start-up
- `js/` — ES modules, no build step:
  - `state.js` (shared state + redraw scheduling), `session.js` (pairing without sending the code (CPace, `cpace.js`) and, with an older driver after a warning, with a key exchange, sealed requests on the home network with a fallback to the key for older drivers, reconnect, 10 s refresh, 401 handling, room rename, key revocation), `controls.js` (optimistic device commands, confirmation by re-reading, door/gate pulse with confirm step), `camera-feed.js` (snapshots as blobs, visible tiles only, paused while hidden; live pictures about every second), `doorbells.js` (noticing rings, Dismiss, notifications), `account.js` (sign in with Google or Apple through api.directorlink.io, only with the sign-ins it says are set up; sign out on this device or everywhere, delete the account; a device that never signed in asks nothing until someone taps Sign in), `lock.js` (the end-to-end lock in WebCrypto, checked against `tests/vectors/lock.json`), `remote.js` (sealed requests through the account and on the home network, linking a home, invitations and their links, and requests to join with another email), `platform.js` (iPhone and iPad), `qr.js` (QR codes, with the vendored MIT `vendor/qrcodegen.js`), `rings.js` (when a ring is recent, relative times; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `setpoints.js` (thermostats with heat and cool setpoints: which ones show, the gap kept between them and the push, what counts as confirmed; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `refrigerators.js` (Samsung refrigerators, 1.7.0: their features, what counts as confirmed and for how long, a scene action's setting; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `reorder.js` (moving one room of the list: where a dragged room lands, the keyboard's moves, auto-scroll near the edges; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `alarm.js` (the alarm's status, read-only: when to ask for it, every 10 s, and its words; unit-tested), `music.js` (Sonos: what each Sonos room plays, read every 5 s while Home or its room is open, play, pause, skip, volume and favorites shown at once and put back when refused, album art through the controller, an admin's room for a Sonos room; unit-tested), `find.js` (Find my controller: where to look, in which order, how many at a time and for how long; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `turn-off.js` (Home's Turn off all: which devices, the second tap, one `POST /v1/off`), `command-parser.js` (Say or type a command: a sentence in English, Hebrew, Spanish or Italian and the user's names to one action, a question or "I didn't understand"; no browser dependencies, unit-tested), `commands.js` (what a command does, through the same calls as a tap, with the second taps), `backup.js` (backups: the file locked with a password in WebCrypto, the strength hint, the parts sent back, the check and the restore), `cloud-backup.js` (automatic backups to the account: the backup password's X25519 key pair, made here with PBKDF2, and a sealed backup opened with it; checked against `tests/vectors/cloud_backup.json`)
  - `model.js` (room names per language, grouping, "on" counts), `favorites.js` (per controller, in `localStorage`), `components.js` (device rows and tiles), `dom.js`, `icons.js` (inline stroke SVG), `theme.js`, `i18n.js`, `pwa.js` (service worker, install prompt), `calendar.js` (the Jewish calendar: whether it is on, reading `GET /v1/calendar`, Hebrew numerals and dates, holiday and weekly-reading names, the lines on Home and Schedules; see Shabbat and holidays)
  - `views/` — `home.js`, `room.js`, `cameras.js`, `climate.js`, `settings.js` (Settings' list and its pages), `connect.js` (first-time setup), `find.js` (Find my controller, under the address), `join.js` (accepting an invitation), `device-join.js` (joining from another device and Join with an invitation, ADR-053; its keys, code and seal are in `js/device-join.js`), `move.js` (Move to the Home Screen app, 1.12.0, ADR-083; `js/home-screen.js` tells a Safari tab from the Home Screen app), `alarm.js` (the alarm on Home and in Settings), `music.js` (a Sonos room's card, Home's Music playing, and the Sonos rooms for the Rooms page), `backup.js` (Settings → Controller → Backup), `cloud-backup.js` (its Automatic backups to your account), `history.js` (Settings → Controller → History), `common.js` (header, connection chip, shared states), `command.js` (Say or type a command: the field on Home, its dialog, the microphone)
- `i18n/en.js`, `i18n/he.js` — interface text
- `styles.css` — the app's styles
- `api-client.js` — shared client for the LAN API (API port, API key storage, pairing code format, `fetch` with Local Network Access annotations); `console/api-client.js` is an exact copy (`scripts/check_sites.py` compares them)
- `_redirects` — sends the old `/console.html` and `/console` to https://console.directorlink.io (301); Cloudflare reads it, it is not published
- `sw.js` — offline mode (see below); it never intercepts controller/LAN requests

## Screens

Hash routes, so Back and reload work: `#/` Home, `#/room/<id>`, `#/cameras`, `#/climate`, `#/settings` and its pages (`#/settings/controller`, `rooms`, `calendar`, `account`, `alerts`, `appearance`, `app`, `about`), `#/join/<invitation>` (taken out of the address as the page opens; see Remote access), `#/access` (Settings → Users; People and devices with a controller before 1.9.0).

## Schedules

Scenes → **Schedules** (`#/schedules`; `js/views/schedules.js`, `js/schedules.js`, `docs/SCHEDULES.md`) lists `GET /v1/schedules`, each in a sentence with its next or last run, and the weather at home from `GET /v1/weather` (read while the screen is open; the controller then keeps it fresh). Admins switch schedules on and off (`PATCH` `enabled`) and edit them at `#/schedule/new` and `#/schedule/{id}`: the scene, when (a time, sunrise/sunset with an offset, or heat/rain/wind with hours and at most once a day; with the Jewish calendar on, also Shabbat: see below), the days, and "only if" weather conditions. Drivers older than 0.14.0 answer 404: the screen says to update the driver.

## Shabbat and holidays

Shabbat and holiday times, the Hebrew date and the week's reading, worked out on the controller (`GET /v1/calendar`, ADR-037; `js/calendar.js`). All of it shows only while `GET /v1/system` says `features.jewish_calendar: true`, which is DirectorLink's **Jewish Calendar** property in Composer (Off by default). A driver before 1.2.0 has no `features`: the app then shows none of it, never asks `/v1/calendar` and never sends `during_shabbat` (such a driver refuses fields it does not know). A `409 JEWISH_CALENDAR_OFF` answer (the property was turned off since `/v1/system` was read) turns it off in the app too until the next read, and says why.

- **Reading** — after connecting, then with the rooms refresh every 10 minutes while the app is in front, on entering Home or Schedules, after saving the settings, and 5 seconds after the next moment the answer changes: the current period's end, the next one's start or end, or today's sunset (from the weather when Schedules has read it, and on the eve of a period from its candle lighting). The answer is `state.calendar`.
- **Home** — under the notices, today's Hebrew date (the next day's from sunset, as the API gives it), its holidays and the week's reading: *18 Tishrei 5787 · Chol HaMoed Sukkot · Shabbat Shmini Atzeret*, *י״ח בתשרי תשפ״ז · חול המועד סוכות · שבת שמיני עצרת*, *24 Tishrei 5787 · Parashat Bereshit*.
- **Schedules** — under the weather, for everyone: the next Shabbat or holiday (its days' names in order: "Shabbat" for a Saturday, then its holy days) with its candle lighting and havdalah, or the one now with its havdalah and the candle lightings still to come in it; "No sunset at this latitude" in polar summer and winter; without a location, where to set it. A footnote gives the minutes and Israel or abroad; admins get **Change**, which opens Settings → Shabbat and holidays.
- **The editor** — a fourth kind of When, **Shabbat**: candle lighting or havdalah (with the next times), and when: from 2 h before to 2 h after (an offset set otherwise through the API, up to 6 h, shows as one more chip). Holy days that follow each other are one period, so it runs once when the period begins or ends; its days are not shown (a new one gets all seven, one made through the API keeps its own). Time, sun and weather schedules get **On Shabbat and holidays**: Run as usual, Not on Shabbat and holidays, or Only on Shabbat and holidays (from candle lighting to havdalah), sent as `during_shabbat` (`run` for a Shabbat trigger, which clears the condition it had). With the calendar off, a Shabbat schedule's editor offers only its switch and Delete, and a condition set earlier is kept and explained.
- **The list** — "30 min before candle lighting", "At havdalah", "Only on Shabbat and holidays"; "Didn’t run today 06:30: Shabbat or a holiday", "Ran today 09:10 (late, after a restart)"; and, by `calendar_status`, "Not running: the Jewish calendar is off in Composer" (or: set the home's location) for Shabbat triggers and "only" schedules, while "not on Shabbat" ones say they run on Shabbat too meanwhile.
- **Settings → Shabbat and holidays** (admins) — Automatic (it says what the home's location gave), As in Israel (one day of Yom Tov) or Abroad (two); candle lighting 0–90 minutes before sunset (18, 20, 30, 40) and havdalah 20–90 after (42, 50, 72). Save sends `PATCH /v1/calendar/settings` with the `version` read; a `409 VERSION_CONFLICT` shows the settings as they are now, to change again.
- **Names** — the API sends only keys and numbers (`HebrewMonth`, `HolidayKey`, parasha ids 1–54); `calendar.months`, `calendar.holidays` and `calendar.parashot` in each dictionary hold the names, and `scripts/check_app.py` fails when one of the API's keys has none. English follows Hebcal's spelling; Hebrew too, except the full spellings מצורע, קדושים, בחוקותי and בהעלותך. Two readings read together are joined with `-` in English and a maqaf (־) in Hebrew (`calendar.join`). Hebrew writes days and years in Hebrew letters (`calendar.numerals`: ט״ו, ט״ז, תשפ״ז, the year without its thousands). `tests/app/calendar.test.mjs` covers the numerals, dates, names, the texts, what the editor offers and sends, the lines for the API's examples (`tests/vectors/calendar/api-examples.json`) and when the calendar is read.

## Scenes

The **Scenes** tab (`js/views/scenes.js`, `js/scenes.js`, `docs/SCENES.md`) lists the home's scenes from `GET /v1/scenes` with a Run button (`POST /v1/scenes/{id}/run`, members and above). An AC action (1.10.0, ADR-070) has **Mode** Off, **On, as it was** (each AC that is off back in its last mode, its temperature and fan its own; with a 1.10.0 driver, `features.climate_last_mode`), Cool, Heat or Auto; **Temperature** Keep or a value; **Fan** Keep or a speed. Keep sends nothing for it (`{"mode": "cool"}` alone); On, as it was hides both (`{"mode": "on"}`). A run that left ACs off because their last mode isn't known yet says so (`NO_LAST_MODE`), and so does History (`counts.no_last_mode`). A level for a room or the whole home goes to its dimmers only with a driver that says `features.scene_levels_dimmers_only` (ADR-077): Add an action says "Only dimmers get a percentage; switches stay as they are.", the action reads "All dimmers", and a run that left only switches (`ON_OFF_ONLY`, `on_off_only`) is done, not partly done. Admins make and change them in the editor (`#/scene/new`, `#/scene/{id}`): name and icon, the actions (Add an action at `#/scene/{id}/add`: where, what, which devices, what to do; since 1.6.0 an action's Edit button, or its text, opens the same screen filled in from it at `#/scene/{id}/edit/{index}`, and Save action puts it back in its place), **Copy the house as it is now**, **Show on Home**, **Try it now** (`POST /v1/scenes/try`) and Save (`POST`/`PATCH /v1/scenes`, with `version`). Scenes marked Show on Home are on Home, above the favorites. Drivers older than 0.13.0 answer 404: the tab says to update the driver.

## Say or type a command

On Home, under the header, a field takes a sentence: type it, or tap the microphone and say it, then Go (or Enter). On wide screens every screen's header has a button for the same field, and `/` opens it anywhere. It understands simple sentences in English, Hebrew, Spanish and Italian by your own names for rooms, devices, scenes and Sonos rooms, only what you may control, on your device and without AI (`js/command-parser.js`, `js/commands.js`, `js/views/command.js`; ADR-063, ADR-066, ADR-068, ADR-079): one thing, or up to five in one sentence, and changes by a step (*brighter*, *warmer*, *louder*, *open the blinds a bit*, *faster*). It shows what it understood ("Kitchen: lights off"), does it the way a tap does, and shows the result. Changing the app's language clears the field and its last answer (their words and examples were in the other language).

- **Languages.** English and Hebrew are understood whatever the app's language (and together, as in 1.9.0: their letters never mix up). Spanish and Italian are understood when the app is in Spanish or Italian, in their own words and first; English and Hebrew then only when the app's language understood nothing in the sentence, and a *no*, a time or a feeling in it stands. So a Spanish sentence is never read as English words (*a 70* is "to 70", never the English article), an English one never as Spanish (*once* is eleven only in Spanish), and Spanish is not Italian. Your names count in every language: a room's Control4 name and its names in each language of Settings → Rooms. The microphone listens in the app's language (es-ES, it-IT).
- **Spanish and Italian grammar.** Articles and contractions (*el*, *la*, *los*, *del*, *al*, *en el*; *il*, *lo*, *gli*, *della*, *nel*, *sul*, *all'*), every imperative, familiar and polite, one or many, and the infinitive (*apaga*, *apague*, *apagad*, *apaguen*, *apagar*, *apagá*; *spegni*, *spenga*, *spegnete*, *spegnere*), with the pronouns they take (*apágalas*, *ciérrala*, *spegnile*, *chiudila*, *spegnerla*), accents or not (*salon*, *ventitre*). *Sube*/*baja* and *alza*/*abbassa* are a step brighter or dimmer for the lights, louder or quieter for the music, the setpoint with *temperatura* or the degrees said, and up or down for the blinds. A state (*encendida*, *abiertas*, *accesa*, *chiuse*, and in Hebrew *דלוק*, *כבוי*, *פתוח*, *סגורים*) says how it is: a question, done nothing with, *?* or not (*סגור* is also the imperative, and closes). *La del salón*, *las de la cocina*, *quella del soggiorno* are the kind said in the part before, in that room (*enciende la luz de la cocina y apaga la del salón*), never the room's All off; with nothing before them, not understood.
- **Numbers** in digits or words: *23*, *23.5*, *twenty three*, *עשרים ושלוש*, *veintitrés*, *treinta y cinco*, *ventitré*, *trentacinque*, *half*, *וחצי*, *y medio*, *e mezzo*, *a la mitad*, *a metà*, and since 1.11.0 *a quarter*, *three quarters*, *רבע*, *שלושה רבעים*, *a un cuarto*, *a tres cuartos*, *a un quarto*, *a tre quarti* (alone, *cuarto* is a room and *quarto* is not a quarter; *at a quarter to seven*, *ברבע*, *en un cuarto de hora*, *tra un quarto d'ora* are times); *%*, *percent*, *por ciento*, *per cento*, *degrees*, *grados*, *gradi*, *°C* or *מעלות*. A number word from one to nine needs its unit (*five percent*, *dos grados*, *cinque per cento*).
- **A time is never a level.** A number right after *at*, *in*, *for*, *until*, *עד*, *a las*, *a la*, *en*, *hasta*, *dentro de*, *por*, *alle*, *all'*, *in*, *tra*, *fra*, *entro*, *per*, *fino a* is a time unless a unit follows (*at 7*, *a las siete*, *en 5*, *alle 7*, *tra 5* are not understood; *at 50%*, *עד 40%*, *en 23 grados* are levels); a number with ב or מ in front of it (*ב-7*, *בשבע*, *ב-20%*, *מ-7*) and *by 20%*, *more*, *less*, *a bit*, *יותר*, *פחות*, *קצת*, *más*, *menos*, *un poco*, *più*, *meno*, *un po'* are a time or a change by that much, never understood alone. Say the level alone (*kitchen lights 30*), or with *to*, *ל*, *על*, *a*, *al*, *su* (*ל-30*, *a 23*, *al 30%*). In Spanish *pon el aire en 23* is not understood (*en 23* may be in 23 minutes): say *a 23* or *en 23 grados*. *Luego*, *después* and *dopo* are later (a time), except right after *y* or *e*: then. *Night*, *בלילה*, *por la noche*, *di notte* are times too, and *a la una 20%* keeps its time.
- **A change by a step** only with its own words: *brighter*, *dimmer*, *darker*, *more light*, *less light*, *dim … a bit*, *תגביר*/*תנמיך* *את האור*, *יותר אור*, *פחות אור*, *sube*/*baja la luz*, *más*/*menos luz*, *más brillo*, *más clara*/*oscura*, *aumenta*/*disminuye*, *alza*/*abbassa la luce*, *più*/*meno luce*, *più luminosità*, *più chiara*/*scura*, *aumenta*/*diminuisci* (lights, 20 points); *warmer*, *cooler*, *יותר חם*/*קר*, *תעלה*/*תוריד את המזגן*, *sube*/*baja la temperatura*, *más caliente*/*frío*, *alza*/*abbassa la temperatura*, *più caldo*/*freddo* (the AC, 1°); *louder*, *quieter*, *turn the music up*/*down*, *volume up*/*down*, *תגביר*/*תנמיך את המוזיקה*, *sube*/*baja la música* or *el volumen*, *más volumen*, *más alta*/*baja*, *alza*/*abbassa la musica* or *il volume*, *più volume*, *più forte*/*piano* (Sonos, 10); since 1.11.0 *open*/*close*/*raise*/*lower … a bit*, *a little*, *a little more*, *more*, *by 20%*, *20% more*, *תפתח*/*תסגור*/*תרים*/*תוריד … קצת*, *טיפה*, *עוד קצת*, *יותר*, *ב-20% יותר*, *abre*/*cierra*/*sube*/*baja … un poco*, *un poco más*, *más*, *un 20%*, *apri*/*chiudi*/*alza*/*abbassa … un po'*, *un po' di più*, *di più*, *del 20%* (blinds, 20 points of their position; never a door or gate, never *less*: Hebrew *ב-20%* with the blind's own verb is *at* as often as *by*, and is not understood), and *faster*, *slower*, *turn the fan up*/*down*, *speed up*, *one speed up*, *two speeds faster*, *תגביר*/*תנמיך*/*תעלה*/*תוריד את המאוורר*, *מהר יותר*, *לאט יותר*, *sube*/*baja el ventilador*, *más rápido*/*lento*, *alza*/*abbassa il ventilatore*, *più veloce*/*lento* (a fan, one of its own speeds). A step's word with a level said with *to*, *ל*, *a*, *al* is that level (*brighter to 80%*, *louder to 40*, *תגביר את האור ל-80%*). An amount goes with its unit and *by*, ב, *un*, *en*, *del*, *di* or the word itself: *by 30%*, *30% brighter*, *2 degrees warmer*, *ב-2 מעלות*, *un 30%*, *en 2 grados*, *del 30%*, *di 2 gradi*; with *sube*/*baja* and *alza*/*abbassa* the degrees or the percent said alone too (*baja el aire dos grados*, *sube la luz 20%*: a level or a temperature is said with *a* or *al*, *sube la luz al 80%*, and no AC is set to 2°). Without its unit an amount is refused, never a level: *brighter by 30*, *ב-7*, *alza il volume di 10*, *sube la luz un 20*; a number alone after *sube* or *alza* (*sube el volumen 10*) is not understood. A comparative with *I*, *me*, *feel*, *לי*, *tengo*, *me*, *hace*, *ho*, *mi*, *fa* is how you feel and does nothing (*make it warmer for me*, *יותר חם לי*, *tengo frío*, *hace calor*, *ho freddo*, *fa caldo*), as is how warm it is (*it's warmer in the bedroom*, *getting colder*, *outside*, *here*, *too*, *נהיה*, *בחוץ*, *פה*, *מדי*, *aquí*, *demasiado*, *qui*, *troppo*). Without the AC said, a comparative is for the one thermostat of the room said, or yours when no room is said (1.11.0: *warmer in the bedroom*, *יותר חם בסלון*, *más calor en el salón*, *più caldo in soggiorno*); with two or more there it asks which one. *Turn up the AC*, *sube el aire* and *alza il condizionatore* are not understood (more cooling, or warmer?); *תעלה את המזגן*, *sube la temperatura* and *alza la temperatura* are warmer. An AC that is off has no setpoint to move (*The AC in Bedroom is off*); one with heat and cool setpoints in auto asks which.
- **Up to five things in one sentence** (three until 1.11.0), parted by *and*, *then*, a comma, *ואז*, *וגם*, *ואת*, ו in front of a verb or a device (*ותסגרו*, *והמזגן*, *ומזגן*), *y*, *e*, *ed*, *poi*, *y luego*, *y después*, *e dopo*, never inside a name or a number (*Rock and Roll*, a room *חדר ורד*, *veintidós y medio*) and never before a room alone (*the lights in the kitchen and the living room* is two rooms at once, as before). A room said once counts for every next part that names none (*kitchen lights off and close the blinds*), and for the part before when they share one verb (*turn off the lights and the AC in the living room*); a verb said once counts for a part that has none (*and the AC*), a step's verb with its *a bit* or *more* (*open the kitchen blinds a bit and the bedroom shutter*), while a step by an amount lends none (the part without one is not clear). A period between two sentences, as dictation writes it, parts them too (*Kitchen lights off. Close the blinds.*); one at the end parts nothing. A room's name inside the name of a device in another room (*רחצה ספוטים כניסה*, a light of the parents' room) is not that room, and is never carried to the next part. It is all or nothing: when one part isn't understood, would ask, is refused or can't be done, nothing is done, and it says which part (*In “close the garage door”: I didn't understand “garage”. Nothing was done.*); a *?* or *¿* anywhere is a question. At most five things; one door or gate; never the same device in two parts (*Two parts change Island*); the whole home's Turn off all in one part while another names a room needs *all* (*turn off all the lights and close the kitchen blinds*), else it asks which room. Each part is shown, then done as a tap would: a door's and Turn off all's second taps (with their own Turn off and Cancel) and a scene that opens doors wait, the others are done at once. *Turn off everything and close the blinds* is one Turn off all with one confirm.
- **Heaters** (lights named for heating: a heater, a boiler, a heat lamp, hot water, floor heating, a towel warmer, *דוד*, *חימום רצפה*, *מחמם מגבות*, *calentador*, *termo*, *calefacción*, *suelo radiante*, *toallero*, *estufa*, *radiador*, *scaldabagno*, *riscaldamento*, *pavimento radiante*, *scaldasalviette*, *stufa*, *termosifone*… whole words; *luz cálida* and *luce calda* are lights) are left as they are by *the lights* of a room or of the home (on, off, a level, brighter or dimmer), a room's All off and Turn off all, and it says so (*The heater “דוד הורים” is left as it is.*); naming one switches it (*turn off the towel warmer*, *תכבו את דוד הורים*, *apaga el termo de los niños*, *spegni lo scaldabagno*). The same rule as Home's Turn off all and a room's All off (`js/heaters.js`).
- **Names** are forgiving: any case, accents and niqqud, Hebrew with or without its prefixes (ב, ה, ל, ו…), singular or plural (*salón*, *salones*), Hebrew spelled with one *י* or *ו* more or less (*חניה*, *חנייה*; *כניסה*, *כנסה*), a small typo, any word order. You need not say a device's kind or "room" (*מצב*, *mode*, *habitación*, *cuarto*, *camera*, *stanza*) when the rest of its name is enough; said with its room word, a numbered room is that room (*the light in room 2*, *la luz de la habitación 2*, not a *Lamp 2* elsewhere), and a room word alone (*the room*, *בחדר*, *en la habitación*, *in camera*) asks which room. A name said only with a typo in a short word, with the other Hebrew plural (*בנים* for *בנות*, *Dana* for *Dina*), or with the other ending of gender (*niñas* for *niños*, *bambini* for *bambine*), asks "Did you mean", since one letter there is often another name.
- **It never guesses.** When two names fit as well, it asks which one (a room and a device of another room named alike: *apaga el termo*; *turn off the heating*, *תכבה את החימום* without *all*: which room); part of a name asks "Did you mean"; a word for a time or "don't" in a name counts only when the whole name is said (a scene *Mañana*: *activa mañana*; *apaga la isla mañana* is a time); when it doesn't understand, it says so with examples made from your own rooms and scenes, and does nothing. It also does nothing for *don't…* (*אל*, *לא*, *no*, *nunca*, *deja*, *non*, *mai*, *lascia*), questions (*is the light on*, *está encendida la luz*, *è accesa la luce*, and anything with a *?* or *¿*: *האור במטבח כבוי?*), times (*at 7 pm*, *בבוקר*, *a las siete*, *mañana*, *alle sette*, *domani*) and how warm you feel (*I'm cold*, *חם לי*, *tengo frío*, *ho caldo*: *hot*, *cold*, *חם*, *קר*, *frío*, *calor*, *freddo*, *caldo* are a mode only right after *on*, *to*, *על*, *en*, *a*, *modo*, *su*, *in*, *modalità*, with the AC said: *מזגן על קר*, *aire en frío*, *condizionatore su freddo*). More than 200 letters is not a command.
- **Doors, gates and Turn off all** keep their second tap: the command shows the button, and only tapping it opens or turns off.
- **The microphone** shows where the browser can turn speech into text (Chrome, Edge, Safari), and listens in the app's language. Your browser's own speech service does it: Chrome sends the sound to Google, Edge to Microsoft, Safari to Apple. DirectorLink sends neither the sound nor the words anywhere. Of the service's guesses the likeliest decides; a less likely one counts only when the likeliest says nothing it understands, never when it says not to or names a time. Closing the dialog or leaving Home while it listens does nothing with what it heard; Stop does it. In the Home Screen app on iPhone and iPad there is no microphone button: tap the keyboard's microphone to dictate into the field (the field stays in place while Home updates, so dictation and the keyboard's composing are not cut).
- **Not understood (yet):** more than five things, one kind in two rooms at once, a fan to a named speed (*fan on high*, *fan speed 3*), *less* for blinds, Hebrew *ב-20%* with a blind's own verb, *turn up the AC* (*sube el aire*, *alza il condizionatore*), *pon el aire en 23*, Spanish or Italian in an app in another language, Sonos favorites and groups, refrigerators, cameras, the alarm and schedules.
- Tests: `tests/app/command-parser.test.mjs` (the sentences), `tests/app/command-parser-es-it.test.mjs` (Spanish and Italian, and which words a sentence is read with; homes in `tests/app/command-homes.mjs`), `tests/app/command.test.mjs` (the field, its requests and second taps, several things, steps, heaters, the field in Spanish and Italian, and since 1.11.0 five things, blinds and fans by a step and the switches' note), `tests/app/command-ios.test.mjs` (the microphone on iPhone), `tests/app/heaters.test.mjs` (which lights are heaters).

## Turn off all

On Home, a list a summary chip filters (lights on, AC on, blinds open) has a button next to **Show all** for members, doors and admins (`js/turn-off.js`; never on the chip itself, and viewers do not get it): *Turn off all 7* (lights), *Turn off all 3* (climate: AC, floor heating and thermostats that are not Off; one without an Off mode is left out), *Close all 4* (blinds that are open and not already closing); with one, *Turn off the light*, *Turn off the AC*, *Close the blind*. It acts on the ones on or open in the rooms the list shows at that moment: rooms you hide stay as they are. Since 1.10.0 it leaves lights named for heating as they are (heaters kept on or off by Composer programming, such as a KNX boiler; `js/heaters.js`, ADR-066): they are not counted, and with its second tap and its result it says so (*Turn off all leaves the heater “דוד הורים” as it is.*); their own switch still turns them off. A room's All off does the same (it says so under its button while a heater is on). Scenes and schedules keep their steps.

- **Second tap** — the first tap makes it *Tap again to turn off 7* (*Tap again to close 4*), with Cancel in place of Show all, for 5 seconds, as a door's Open button does. There is no undo.
- **One request** — the second tap sends `POST /v1/off` with `type` and `device_ids` (members; 1.3.0): lights off, thermostats to Off (as a scene's All AC: Off), blinds to 0. A driver before 1.3.0 answers 404; each device then gets its own `PATCH`, all at once.
- **Results** — *Turning off…* (*Closing…*), then *Done* for a few seconds. Lights and AC show off at once, blinds show *Closing…* and are read every 2 s until they stop, and the devices are read again 1.5 s later, so the chips' counts follow. Devices that did not turn off are named with their room for 15 s (the first 8, the rest counted), and the button is there again for them; a request that failed says why and changes nothing.
- The button goes when nothing in the list is on or open any more; the filter stays. `tests/app/turn-off.test.mjs` covers the count and words, the second tap, its 5 seconds and Cancel, viewers, which devices are named, partial failures, the requests, and the heaters left (Home's and a room's All off).

## Profiles

Each user's language, theme, palette, favorites and hidden rooms are kept on the controller, in their profile, and shared by their devices (`js/profile.js`, `docs/PREFERENCES.md`). After connecting, and every minute, the app reads `GET /v1/profile` and applies it; the first time, this browser's own choices become the profile's; changes go back with `PATCH /v1/profile` a moment later. The browser keeps a copy, so the app opens in the right language before it reaches the controller and works as before with drivers older than 0.12.0. Settings → Rooms: untick a room to hide it for yourself; admins set the home's order by dragging a room by its handle (on a touch screen, held first; a swipe that starts on it still scrolls), with the keyboard (Space or Enter picks it up, the arrow keys, Home and End move it, Space or Enter puts it down, Escape puts it back; the place is read out), or one place at a time with the arrows. Each move is one `PUT /v1/rooms/order`; moves made while one is on its way go together in the next, and a refusal puts the order back. **Add my other device** makes the new device join your profile.

- **Home** — a doorbell banner while someone rings (see Doorbells), connection chip, the Hebrew date and the week's reading (with the Jewish calendar on; see Shabbat and holidays), the alarm (see Alarm), summary chips ("2 lights on", "1 AC on", "1 blind open"; tapping one filters the rooms, and the filtered list has **Turn off all**: see below), Favorites (Edit mode to add, remove and reorder), room cards. Without a key it shows the connect screen: controller address and **pairing code** (see below).
- **Room** — All off (lights and AC; lights named for heating stay as they are), then Lights, Climate, Blinds, Refrigerators (drivers with `/v1/refrigerators`, 1.7.0: the fridge's and freezer's temperatures, the door, the water filter, and a switch per feature that says "Turning on…" until the refrigerator confirms through Samsung's cloud, read every 2 s for up to 65 s, then quietly every 5 s for 2 minutes more, so that a late confirmation still shows), Doorbells (drivers with `/v1/doorbells`), Doors & gates (drivers with `/v1/relays`; since 1.10.0 each says what it is, Door, Gate, Garage door or Door or gate, and open or closed when its Relay Door, Gate or Garage Door Controller has a contact, ADR-069), Cameras (without the doorbell's own camera, shown with the doorbell) and the room's other, uncontrollable devices. Empty sections are hidden; the star on each device adds it to Favorites.
- **Cameras** — one large picture and a grid; thumbnails refresh about every 3 s, the full view about every second.
- **Climate** — all thermostats grouped by room. A thermostat with heat and cool setpoints (`setpoints: dual`, drivers from 1.1.0) has a Heat or a Cool stepper in heat and cool, and both in auto and off (stacked on phones); raising heat into the gap moves cool up and lowering cool moves heat down, and the taps go out as one `PATCH` with `heat_setpoint` and `cool_setpoint`.
- **Settings** — a row for each page, with a line saying how things are ("2 rooms, 1 hidden", "Connected · Up to date"; a badge on Controller when a newer DirectorLink is out): **Controller** (`#/settings/controller`: address, status, the project; admins: History, see below; Updates with the app's and DirectorLink's versions, Check now and how to update; admins: Backup, see below; Pair again, Forget access key), **Rooms** (`#/settings/rooms`: which rooms you see; admins: the home's order and the names per language, `PATCH /v1/rooms/{id}`; then the Sonos rooms, admins with Sonos on: see Music), **Shabbat and holidays** (`#/settings/calendar`, admins, with the Jewish calendar on), **Users** (`#/access`; every user since 1.9.0, admins before), **Account** (`#/settings/account`: sign in with Google or Apple, which comes back here, sign out, add or remove a sign-in, delete account, and This home: link it, Add my other device, Invite someone with a controller before 1.9.0 (Settings → Users → Add a user since then), and on a Safari tab of iPhone or iPad, Move to the Home Screen app; `docs/ACCOUNTS.md`). Since 1.12.0 a user still named after a device is asked "What's your name?" once, at the top of the list, **Appearance and language** (`#/settings/appearance`, 1.10.0: the language, the theme and colours, which follow the user, and this device's text size; see Palettes, themes and text size, and Languages), **Alerts** (`#/settings/alerts`, 1.10.0, between Account and Appearance and language: this device's alerts, see Alerts; its line says Off, On with how many kinds, or what they still need, such as Sign in to get alerts), **App** (offline copy, install, API console — opens https://console.directorlink.io, or `http://127.0.0.1:8081` when the app runs on localhost; doorbell notifications) and **About**. Each page has Back to the list, which then focuses the row that opened it. The controller's project counts include doorbells.

## Alarm

Read-only (docs/DECISIONS.md, ADR-038). Only when `GET /v1/system` says `features.alarm_status` (an installer set **Alarm Status** to On in Composer) and only for member, doors and admin keys does the app ask `GET /v1/alarm` (sealed, like every request), after connecting and every 10 s. Home then shows each partition the alarm uses — its name and room, and "Armed away", "Disarmed, not ready", "Entry delay", "Alarm: fire"… with open zones, the seconds a delay has left (counted down between reads) and trouble — and Settings → Controller has an Alarm line. There is nothing to press: the app never arms or disarms. With Alarm Status off, and for viewers, nothing is asked or shown; a refusal (`403`, e.g. a request that could not be sealed) or an older driver (`404`) shows nothing; without an answer the last status stays while Home says the controller cannot be reached.

## Music (Sonos)

Only when `GET /v1/system` says `features.sonos` (an installer set **Sonos** to On in Composer; docs/SONOS.md, ADR-044). After connecting the app reads `GET /v1/music` once, then every 5 s while Home (all rooms) or a room (`?room_id=`) is open, and not on other screens. A room's screen has a Music card for each Sonos room shown there: album art (`GET /v1/music/{id}/art`, as a blob like camera pictures), what plays, its state and group; members and admins get previous, play or pause and next (left to right in Hebrew too, as media controls are), the volume and mute, and the Sonos favorites, which load when opened and, after an error, with Retry (those only the Sonos app starts are greyed out). Home lists each group that plays, with a pause button. A Sonos room whose name matches no room is under No room until an admin picks its room (`musicRoomsSection()` in `js/views/music.js`, on Settings → Rooms); the first choice, "Same name: Kitchen" or "Not in a room", puts it back to matching by name. A command shows at once on every room of the group and is put back, with a short error, when the speaker refuses it. Scenes have a Music action (pause or stop, in a room or the whole home).

## Backup

Settings → Controller → **Backup**, for admins (`js/backup.js`, `js/views/backup.js`, `docs/BACKUP.md`, ADR-042). **Download backup** asks for a password twice (10 characters at least, with a strength hint, and says that without it the file cannot be opened), reads `GET /v1/backup`, locks it here with WebCrypto (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations, a random salt; AES-256-GCM, a random IV, the header authenticated) and saves "DirectorLink backup <home> <date>.dlbackup". **Restore from backup** takes the file and its password, opens it here (a wrong password, or a changed file, opens nothing and sends nothing), sends it in parts (`POST /v1/restore/parts`, each under 30 KB of JSON so that it fits a sealed request) and shows the controller's check (`POST /v1/restore`): first, when the backup looks like another home's, a warning naming it and why; the date and version, the counts, the keys that come back by name and role (or that the keys are kept as they are now), devices found by name, what is not in the project and where it was used, what is left out, remote access, and the Composer settings to set by hand. Where the backup's keys come back, **This device is** lets the admin pick the key this device had before (`replaces_key`); for another home's backup, **Move remote access to this controller** (`move_remote`) is off unless ticked. **Replace everything**, confirmed with the home's name, restores it (`"dry_run": false`; an upload the controller no longer has is sent again), then the app reads everything again, and a device linked through the account to another home is pointed at the backup's. The password never leaves the browser, and is never written into the page's HTML; the opened document goes only to this controller, in sealed requests. The passwords, the file and the opened backup stay in `js/views/backup.js` (never in storage or `ui`) and go when the panel closes or the key is forgotten. A file over 4 MB is not read. The panel shows only when `GET /v1/system` says `features.backup` (a DirectorLink from 1.4.0; an older one has no backups); a `404` from the controller says to update DirectorLink in Composer. `tests/app/backup.test.mjs` covers the file, the parts and the panel. The preview counts the Sonos rooms when the backup has them (1.6.0).

**Automatic backups to your account** (1.6.0, ADR-048; `js/cloud-backup.js`, `js/views/cloud-backup.js`), in the same card, for admins signed in to their account, with a DirectorLink that says `features.automatic_backup`. **Set backup password** asks for it twice, with the same strength hint, says plainly that without it the backups cannot be opened, makes an X25519 key pair from it here (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations, a random salt) and sends the controller only the public key, the salt and the iterations (`PUT /v1/backup/automatic`, sealed); then it backs up at once (`POST /v1/backup/automatic/run`) and asks the controller every 3 s until that is done. The card says when the nightly backup runs (the controller's time) and how the last one went, with **Back up now**, **Change backup password** (backups made before keep their old password; the list marks them "earlier password") and **Turn off** (the account's backups stay). Below, the backups in the account (`GET /v1/homes/{home_id}/backups` for each of the account's homes it may see, this device's linked home first): date and size, **Restore…** and **Delete these backups**. Restore asks for the backup password, downloads the backup, opens it here (a wrong password opens nothing and sends nothing) and hands the document to the same check, preview and Replace everything as a file. The passwords stay in `js/views/cloud-backup.js`, never in storage or `ui`. `tests/app/cloud-backup.test.mjs` covers the key, the shared vectors and the section.

## History

Settings → Controller → **History**, for admins (`#/settings/history`; `js/views/history.js`, `docs/HISTORY.md`, ADR-046). It reads `GET /v1/activity` 50 entries at a time (**Load more** asks `before` the last one shown), newest first, a section a day in the home's time zone (Today, Yesterday, then the date): an icon per kind, what happened and who in one line (a key's device and its user, a schedule as Schedules says it, Composer or DirectorLink), the time, and how it went (what ran, what was skipped and why in plain words, what failed). Names from Control4 keep their own direction. The chips (a group of toggle buttons) show All, Scenes and schedules, Doors, Changes in Composer or Access. While open, the newest are read again every 30 s and go on top; a screen reader hears how many entries are shown. Alert notifications open `#/settings/history`; for a key that is not an admin's it shows Settings' list. A DirectorLink before 1.6.0 answers `404`: the page says to update it. Back leads to Settings → Controller with its History link focused. `tests/app/history.test.mjs` covers the link, the page, the chips, Load more and Hebrew.

## Pairing

The pairing code is the only way to get a device's first key. In Composer: DirectorLink → Actions → **New Pairing Code** (one is also made when the driver is added or has no keys); Composer shows it as `1234 5678`. A code lasts 15 minutes and works once. The field brings up the number pad (`inputmode="numeric"`, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`) and formats the code as it is typed or pasted, with or without the space or a dash. The code is never sent: the app pairs with CPace (`js/cpace.js`, docs/DECISIONS.md, ADR-039), two requests to `POST /v1/auth/pair` that give an admin key sealed for this exchange; the name is the browser and system, e.g. "Chrome on Windows". A driver before 1.3.0 refuses the field `cpace`: the connect screen then warns that the code would travel over the network unprotected and that DirectorLink should be updated in Composer, and only **Pair anyway** sends `{"pairing_code", "name", "exchange"}` (`pairSealed` in `session.js`: sealed for this device's X25519 key exchange; browsers without X25519, and drivers before 1.0.0, pair without `exchange` and get the key in the clear). Every problem it can answer has its own message in each language: `INVALID_FIELD` (not 8 digits), `PAIRING_CODE_INVALID` (with the tries left), `PAIRING_NOT_ACTIVE` (no code — run New Pairing Code), `PAIRING_RATE_LIMITED` (5 wrong codes lock pairing for this device for 60 s; 20 in all close the code, then `PAIRING_NOT_ACTIVE`), `KEY_LIMIT_REACHED`, `PAIRING_UNAVAILABLE`, `PAIRING_SESSION_EXPIRED` (the exchange took over a minute), `PAIRING_CODE_EXPIRED` (a new code was made meanwhile), and a controller that could not prove it knows the code. More keys, with other roles, are created by an admin in the API console (Keys).

### Find my controller

Under the address field (`js/find.js`, `js/views/find.js`). A web page cannot learn this device's address or network, and browsers cannot look for devices by name (mDNS, SSDP), so the app asks `GET /v1/health` on port 41999, which needs no key, at the addresses homes use most. It sends nothing else: no key, no pairing code, and each request is sent as `api-client.js` sends them (`targetAddressSpace: "local"`, see Local Network Access).

- **Where** — first the address this browser used before, if any, and the routers' usual addresses (x.1, x.254 and x.138, Bezeq's) of each network: a network whose router answers, even to refuse, is looked through first. Then, in this order: the used address's network, 192.168.1.x, 192.168.0.x, 10.0.0.x (Bezeq), 10.0.1.x, 192.168.2.x, 192.168.10.x, 192.168.50.x, 10.1.1.x, 172.16.0.x, each from .1 to .254.
- **How** — at most 64 requests at a time; an address that does not answer within 1.2 s is given up (a router within 2 s). The whole search takes 30 seconds at most: a request that could not end within them is not started, so the last networks may not be reached. The line under the button says what it is doing ("Looking in 192.168.1.x…"); the button becomes Cancel, which drops every request on its way.
- **Recognising it** — the health answer's `product: "directorlink"` (drivers from 1.3.0); older drivers by their answer's fields (`status`, `version`, `api_version: "1"`, `detail`). The browser reads an answer only when the device allows the app's origin (CORS), so other devices on port 41999 go unread.
- **Found** — its network is looked through to the end, then the search stops. One: it goes into the address field ("Found DirectorLink 1.3.0 at 192.168.1.50") and the pairing code field comes next; an address typed meanwhile is not replaced. Several: they are listed with their versions to pick from. The person still types the code and taps Connect.
- **Not found** — "No controller found on this network. Type the controller's address. Your installer can tell you, or find it in Composer."
- **The browser's question** — while Chromium asks whether the site may reach the local network, every request waits: the first ones are sent once, the search waits up to 30 s for the answer ("Allow access to devices on your local network when the browser asks"), then starts. Refused: it says how to allow it in the site's settings.
- **Where it is offered** — only where pairing at home works: Chromium browsers on computers and Android (`findSupported`). Not on iPhone and iPad, whose pairing screen sends people through the account, nor in Firefox or Safari.

`tests/app/find-controller.test.mjs` covers the order, the routers first, 64 at a time, the timeouts, the 30 s, Cancel, one, several or no controllers, the browser's question, what is sent and the browsers it is offered in. To try it with the dev server (Local development), make it look on this computer from the browser's console, then tap Find my controller: it finds 127.0.0.1.

```js
(await import("/js/find.js")).scanSettings.ranges = ["127.0.0"];
```

Admins and members (DirectorLink 1.8.0, ADR-054): `GET /v1/api-keys/current` says what this user may do (`access`, kept in `state.access`), and `can()` follows it — an admin everything; a member uses what the controller lists for them (it lists nothing else: their rooms and kinds, the scenes chosen for them), opens doors and gates if given them, sees the alarm if given it, and has no schedules (`views/schedules.js`), scene editing, History or controller settings. Settings → Users (People and devices before 1.9.0) sets each user's role and, for a member, their rooms, kinds, cameras, doors and gates, the alarm and the scenes they may run (`views/permissions.js`); Add a user (Invite someone before 1.12.0) offers the same; Settings → Rooms hides a room from members. `tests/app/people.test.mjs` covers them.

API key roles (drivers from v0.7.0 to 1.7.0): on connect the app reads `GET /v1/api-keys/current` (404 on older drivers → treated as admin) and shows only what the key may do — `viewer` sees state without controls, `member` controls lights, climate and blinds, `doors` also opens doors and gates (after a confirming second tap), `admin` also renames and orders rooms, makes scenes and schedules, and manages users, their devices and invitations. A 403 `FORBIDDEN` reverts the change, shows "Your access level (…) can't do this" and adopts the role it reports; `DOOR_CONTROL_DISABLED` explains how to turn Door Control on in Composer. Forget key and Pair again revoke the key with `DELETE /v1/api-keys/current` (older drivers: the key list); a 401 on any request forgets the key and asks for a new pairing code.

## Doorbells

DoorBird doorstations come from `GET /v1/doorbells`, polled with the other devices every 10 s (an older driver answers 404: no doorbells; other failures keep the last list). Since DirectorLink 1.10.0 a doorbell camera (a camera whose driver follows DirectorLink's camera agreement and says it is a doorbell, ADR-065, docs/CAMERA_DRIVERS.md) is listed there too, under the camera's id, its `camera` itself and `can_open` false: the app shows it as any doorbell, and its camera's tile stays out of the room it is shown in.

- **Ringing** — a ring is recent for 2 minutes: when its `last_ring_at` is within 2 minutes of this browser's clock (a controller clock up to 2 minutes ahead is believed), or when this page saw `last_ring_at` change within the last 2 minutes, whatever the clocks say. While it is recent, Home starts with a banner — "Someone is at the door — <name>", the doorbell's camera refreshed about every second, **Open gate** and **Dismiss** — and the other screens show one line that leads to it. Dismiss remembers that `last_ring_at` (per controller, in `localStorage`); the next ring shows the banner again.
- **Open gate** — doors and admin keys, on doorbells with `can_open`: a second tap within 5 s sends `POST /v1/doorbells/{id}/open` (Opening… → Sent). `403 DOOR_CONTROL_DISABLED` explains the Composer switch.
- **Room** — the picture (tap for the full view), "Last ring: 3 minutes ago · Motion: …", "Not responding" after a communication failure, the last five events with relative times (`Intl.RelativeTimeFormat`), and Open gate.
- **Favorites** — a starred doorbell shows its last ring and opens its room; it is highlighted while ringing.
- **Notifications** — Settings → App → **Turn on doorbell notifications** asks for the permission (so does Alerts, below). A ring then notifies (through the service worker when there is one) while the app is open but not in front; in the background only `/v1/doorbells` is polled. With the app closed, a ring comes as an alert (below, DirectorLink 1.7.0), under the same tag (`doorbell-<id>`) with the ring's time: a ring one of them shows already is not shown twice. Clicking the notification brings the app to Home.

Controls change the screen at once, send the command, then re-read the device until the controller confirms it; a failed command reverts and shows a short error on the device. Device state refreshes every 10 s while the page is visible.

## Alerts

Notifications with the app closed (Web Push; 1.6.0, ADR-047; 1.7.0, ADR-050): someone rang at a door ("Front gate rang at 08:00."), a door or gate was opened ("Main door was opened by Dana (Dana's iPhone) at 08:01."; admins, if they choose), the refrigerator door was left open, a schedule had a problem (admins), the home has been offline for 10 minutes (admins). The controller decides who gets what (each key's role and its own choices) and seals what happened to each device's key; the account service only delivers it. Tapping a ring opens Home, a refrigerator's alert its room, the others Settings → Controller → History (`#/settings/history`). With a controller before 1.7.0 (no `features.alert_choices`), as in 1.6.0: admins only, the offline and schedule alerts, which say only their kind and time.

- **The switch** — Settings → **Alerts** → **Alerts on this device** (`#/settings/alerts`, `js/views/alerts.js`; on Settings → Controller before 1.10.0), for everyone signed in to an account on a device linked to the home (else it says to link it; signed out, the page offers to sign in and comes back to it); admins only with a controller before 1.7.0, and a member there has no Alerts row. Once on, a switch per kind the controller offers this key (`GET` and `PUT /v1/alerts/choices`, sealed), and for admins the offline alert, which the account service keeps (`offline` in the registration). Turned on, it asks for the permission (the only other place that does is doorbell notifications), subscribes the browser with the account service's public key (`GET /v1/homes/{home_id}/alerts`, `pushManager.subscribe` with `userVisibleOnly`), registers it with this device's key id (`POST`, `key_id`) and tells the controller (`PUT /v1/alerts/choices` `{"on": true}`; a controller that cannot be told leaves alerts off). Off unregisters it (`DELETE`), drops the subscription and tells the controller. The account service refuses an account that has not used this device's key at the home yet (`KEY_NOT_LINKED`: the app first tells it with one sealed request through the account, then asks again; when that request does not reach the home, it says the home could not be reached). Signing out, Sign out everywhere, deleting the account and forgetting the key end this browser's alerts too.
- **iPhone and iPad** — only the app added to the Home Screen gets them, on iOS 16.4 or later. In Safari the card says how (Share → Add to Home Screen); added on an older iOS, which version it needs.
- **Each start** — signed in, the app registers again (`js/alerts.js`): the browser may have a new subscription, the account service a new key (the app then subscribes again), or the device may no longer be allowed (the switch turns off and says why). A controller that does not know this device has alerts on (switched on by an app before 1.7.0, or restored) is told.
- **The words** — the service worker (`sw.js`, `push`) opens what the controller sealed to this device with its alert key, `HMAC-SHA256(lock key, "DirectorLink alert v1")`, which `js/alerts.js` keeps for it in Cache Storage (`directorlink-alerts`, `/alert-key.json`, with the home and key id; never the lock key or the API key), checking the MAC first (`tests/vectors/alert.json`). It writes the sentence with the app's own words in the app's language, kept there too and written again when the language changes; the time is this device's. The account service's offline alert, and anything that does not open, get the general words (“Your home – …”). Without the app's words, English. Every push shows a notification: a ring the app already shows, or shows on its banner because it is in front, is shown again quietly in its place.

## Updates

For admin keys, the app says when a newer DirectorLink is out (`js/updates.js`, `js/views/updates.js`, ADR-035). At most every 12 hours it asks GitHub's releases API (`https://api.github.com/repos/DirectorLink/DirectorLink/releases/latest`, without cookies; the CSP's `connect-src https:` allows it) and keeps the answer in `localStorage`. Settings → Controller shows **Updates**: *Up to date*, or the newer version and its date with **Download DirectorLink.c4z** (that release's file), **What's new** (the release page, in a new tab) and the steps in Composer; Home shows a notice until it is dismissed for that version. Other roles never ask, nor does a driver whose version is not MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH (`dev`); a failed request keeps the last answer and its time until the next try. *Up to date* is said only within 3 days of GitHub's last answer, then *Could not check for updates* with that day; a newer release already known stays offered. Only immutable releases are offered (their files cannot be replaced), and only the version, the date and links into the project's releases are used, never the text of the release notes. The rooms refresh, once a minute, reads `GET /v1/system` again, so after Update Driver in Composer the line says *Up to date* without a reload. `tests/app/updates.test.mjs` covers the comparison, the 12 hours, the 3 days, the admin-only rule and the answers that are refused; `tests/app/update-notice.test.mjs` the screens and the refresh. **Check now** (1.5.0), under Settings → Controller, asks GitHub at once, at most once a minute, and the 12 hours start again from it; Settings also shows the app's own version (`js/version.js`, kept equal to the release's VERSION by `scripts/check_app.py`).

## Palettes, themes and text size

Five palettes — graphite (default), ocean, forest, plum, midnight — each with a light and a dark variant, as CSS custom properties on `:root[data-palette=…][data-theme=…]` in `styles.css` (tokens `--bg`, `--card`, `--nav`, `--ink`, `--muted`, `--line`, `--onBg`/`--onText`, `--coolBg`/`--coolText`, `--primary`/`--primaryText`, `--okBg`/`--okText`/`--okDot`). The theme is Light, Dark or Auto (follows `prefers-color-scheme`); `data-theme` always holds the resolved value. The choice is stored in `localStorage` (`directorlink.palette`, `directorlink.theme`) and `meta[name=theme-color]` follows the page background. Adding a palette: a light and a dark token block and a swatch rule in `styles.css`, its name in `PALETTES` (`js/theme.js`) and `theme-boot.js`, and a label under `palettes` in each language file.

The text size (1.10.0, ADR-067) is Small, Default, Large or Larger: `data-text-size` on `<html>` sets `--text-scale` (0.9375, 1, 1.125, 1.25), the root font size is `calc(100% * var(--text-scale))`, and every font size in `styles.css` is in `rem`, so every screen scales; spacing, icons and the layout stay as they are, and text wraps. Page titles of the app wrap instead of being cut (the actions go under a word that does not fit), buttons wrap long labels, camera names take two lines, and the tab labels stop growing at 13px so that five tabs fit a 320px screen. It is this device's only (`localStorage` `directorlink.textSize`, `js/theme.js`), set before the first paint by `theme-boot.js`; `tests/app/appearance.test.mjs` covers the page, what is saved where, and the boot script.

Fonts are Sora (headings) and IBM Plex Sans / IBM Plex Sans Hebrew (text) from Google Fonts, with system fallbacks — the offline copy renders without them.

## Languages

Every string on screen goes through `t(key, params)` from `js/i18n.js`, with plural forms chosen by `Intl.PluralRules` (`{ one: …, two: …, other: … }`). Language is Auto (the browser's first language the app has, else English), English, עברית, Español or Italiano (1.10.0, ADR-067), stored as `directorlink.lang` and in the user's profile. Hebrew sets `<html lang="he" dir="rtl">`; the layout uses logical CSS properties, so it mirrors, and directional icons flip. Numbers, dates, times and "3 minutes ago" come from `Intl` in the language shown. Names from Control4 are rendered with `dir="auto"`. Rooms show `room.names[lang]` when set in Settings → Rooms (a field per language), else the Control4 name.

Spanish and Italian use the informal *tú* and *tu*, as home apps do, and every string is translated: `scripts/check_app.py` fails when a language file lacks a key of `en.js` or has one more, when a string's `{placeholders}` differ from English (a plural's forms together; `command.example.*` may give the room as `{inRoom}`, as Hebrew does), when a plural lacks `other` (or `zero` where English has one), and unless the disclaimer is word for word ("DirectorLink es un proyecto independiente, sin afiliación con Control4 ni Snap One.", "DirectorLink è un progetto indipendente, non affiliato a Control4 né a Snap One."). `tests/app/languages.test.mjs` checks the same, renders the main screens in Spanish and Italian, and checks their numbers, dates, alerts and the microphone's language. In Spanish and Italian the Jewish calendar's names are the Hebrew names as English spells them; Composer's names, the API console, the driver's Composer documentation and the website stay in English.

Say or type a command shows in every language, with its words in that language, and the microphone listens in it (`speech` in `LANGUAGES`: en-US, he-IL, es-ES, it-IT); `js/command-parser.js` understands English and Hebrew sentences in every language, and Spanish and Italian ones in Spanish and Italian (ADR-068; a language's command words are a lexicon of their own there, and an app language without one understands English and Hebrew).

To add a language:

1. Copy `i18n/en.js` to `i18n/<code>.js` and translate every value (`check_app.py` requires all of them).
2. Add one line to `LANGUAGES` in `js/i18n.js`: `{ code: "<code>", label: "<name in that language>", dir: "ltr" | "rtl", speech: "<recognition language>" }`, the code to `languages` in `theme-boot.js` (and to `rtl` there for a right-to-left one), and the file to `ASSETS` in `sw.js` (an update replaces the offline copy, so every language is saved at install).
3. Write the disclaimer in `DISCLAIMERS` in `scripts/check_app.py`.

## Offline mode

The service worker saves the app on the device, so it still opens when the internet is down and keeps controlling the house over the LAN:

- every same-origin `GET` goes to the network first and falls back to the saved copy when the network fails or takes longer than 3 seconds; each successful load refreshes the saved copy, so it never goes stale
- pages are saved under every path that serves them — Cloudflare redirects `/index.html` → `/` — and stored without the redirect, because browsers refuse redirected responses for page loads; redirects to other sites (the old console addresses) are left to the browser and never saved
- controller requests are cross-origin and are never intercepted or cached
- Settings → App → **Offline copy** shows whether the app is saved

`tests/app/sw.test.mjs` checks this against a fake network that serves the app the way Cloudflare does. Tests live outside `app/` because Cloudflare publishes everything in this folder.

## Cloudflare

The app is the Cloudflare Workers static-assets project `directorlink-app` on the `app.directorlink.io` custom domain ([`wrangler.jsonc`](wrangler.jsonc): assets from `.`, Cloudflare's default HTML and 404 handling). `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs `wrangler deploy` from this folder on pushes to `main`, after the owner approves the run (ADR-075; pull requests get only a dry run, no preview versions), and first writes `build.json` here: the commit and the time, which `scripts/verify_live.mjs` compares with the source.

`_headers` sets the security headers, `_redirects` the old console addresses; `.assetsignore` keeps `wrangler.jsonc`, `.assetsignore` and this README from being published. No environment variables are required. Every merge to `main` that the owner approves deploys, so web changes must go out together with the driver version they need.

## Local development

The driver answers browsers only from `https://app.directorlink.io` and `https://console.directorlink.io` (since 1.0.0, not `localhost`), so a local copy of the app talks to the fake controller of the dev server, which allows local origins:

```bash
python scripts/dev_server.py                  # fake controller on http://localhost:41999
python -m http.server 8080 --directory app    # app on http://localhost:8080
```

Then open `http://localhost:8080`, use `localhost` as the controller address and the pairing code the dev server prints (see `docs/BUILD.md`). App changes reach a real controller once they are deployed.

## Local Network Access

The production site is served over HTTPS and talks to the controller over plain HTTP on the LAN. Chromium browsers gate these requests behind Local Network Access permission; requests to private IP literals or `.local` hostnames, annotated with `targetAddressSpace: "local"`, are allowed after the user grants it. A controller on this computer (`localhost`, the dev server) is annotated `"loopback"` instead, because Chromium blocks a request whose annotation does not match the address.

**iPhone and iPad cannot use the LAN connection.** Every browser on iOS and iPadOS uses WebKit, which blocks these HTTP requests from an HTTPS page as mixed content and has no Local Network Access permission to allow them; the request never leaves the device (the controller logs nothing) and the app shows "Could not reach DirectorLink". Pairing therefore has to happen on a computer or an Android phone. iPhones and iPads connect through the account instead (Remote access, below). The app added to the Home Screen has its own storage and never receives links (iOS opens them in Safari): it joins with **Join from another device**, or with **Join with an invitation** (Paste invitation link, 1.7.0, below). Since 1.12.0 (ADR-083) an invitation's link opened in Safari first recommends joining in the Home Screen app (Copy link; Share → Add to Home Screen; Join with an invitation there), and a Safari tab that joined moves its place there (Settings → Account → Move to the Home Screen app: a move invitation for the same user, whose key goes when the Home Screen app first uses its own).

## Remote access

With an account (`docs/ACCOUNTS.md`), requests are sealed on the device with its lock key (`js/lock.js`) and sent to `api.directorlink.io`, which passes them to the home without being able to read them. On the home network the same sealed envelopes go to the controller itself (`POST /v1/sealed`), with or without an account.

- **Link this home** (Settings → Account, on the home network, admin key, Remote Access On in Composer): the app asks the controller for a claim token and gives it to the account. A home already linked to this account is only linked for this device; one linked to another account is taken over only after a confirmation.
- **Add my other device** / **Add a user**'s Send a link (Invite someone before 1.12.0): the controller makes an invitation (10 minutes, or 7 days for an email; since 1.12.0 with the new user's name, which it keeps and tells only the joining device, sealed: "You joined Cohen Home as Dana.") and registers its id, email and expiry with the account over its own connection (drivers before 1.0.0: the app registers it, for the home's owner only), and the app shows the link and a QR code: `https://app.directorlink.io/#/join/<home>.<invitation>.<secret>`. The secret never reaches a server.
- **Joining** (`#/join`): sign in with the invited email and accept; the controller's new key comes back sealed. It replaces a key the device already had, after a confirmation. Signed in with another email (Apple's Hide My Email, another account), the page asks the home's owner and shows a 6-digit code to read out to them; it asks every 5 seconds and finishes by itself once they approve (ADR-041). A refusal or the invitation's expiry is said; the request can be withdrawn.
- **Join from another device** (1.7.0, ADR-053; `js/views/device-join.js`, `js/device-join.js`): on the Connect screen, signed in, a device without a key asks to join one of the account's homes (`POST /v1/homes/{home}/device-requests` with its label, e.g. *Home Screen app on iPhone*, and a commitment, the SHA-256 of its X25519 public key). A device of the same account linked to the home with an admin key (its role read from the controller first; since 1.9.0, with a controller that says `features.users`, any key: every user approves their own new devices, ADR-061, and a user who has five devices is shown "Remove a device first" with the list) looks for requests every 60 s while it is shown, at once when it comes to the front or connects, and every 2 s only while it answers one, and shows them under the header of every screen. **Show code** sends its own public key; the new device then shows its key (checked against the commitment) and its 6-digit code from the request and both keys, which the person types on the approving device (it works the code out too and never shows it). **Approve** with the same code makes the invitation of Add my other device at the controller and seals it to the new device (X25519, HKDF-SHA-256, AES-256-GCM); a wrong code approves nothing, and the third declines the request. If Approve's answer is lost, the request is read before the invitation is revoked (kept when the cloud took it). The new device collects it once, opens it and joins as with the link. **Decline** ends it. A key saved on the new device another way (pairing, an invitation's link) withdraws its request when it connects. The new device keeps its request (and its key pair's secret, for 10 minutes at most) in `localStorage` so a reload carries on, and gives up if the approving key changes after it showed its own. Offered only when `GET /v1/me` says `device_requests: true` (account service 1.7.0); errors say what to do (no device that could approve, too many requests, declined or ran out).
- **Paste invitation link** (the Connect screen; Settings → Account): reads the clipboard (`navigator.clipboard.readText`; iOS shows its Paste button) and, when that is refused, missing or holds no link, shows a field. A whole link, a message with one in it, or only the part after `#/join/`; it then opens the join page as the link would.
- **Choosing the connection**: the home network first; when it gives no answer, the account (reads are sent again that way, commands are not, so a gate never opens twice). Away from home the app checks once a minute whether the home network is back, with `GET /v1/sealed`, which needs no key (`GET /v1/remote` for drivers before 1.0.0), and returns only when the controller there is this home. iPhone and iPad always use the account. The chip says **Connected · via account**.
- Failures of the account connection (signed out, home offline) never forget the device's key; signed out, the banner offers to sign in again.
- **Users** (`#/access`, a row on Settings' list; DirectorLink 1.9.0, ADR-061, `features.users`): `GET /v1/users`, each user with their access, whether their devices use a Google or Apple account, and under them every device with when it was last used and Remove where the controller says the caller may (a member sees only their own user); for admins, Pair a device (a pairing code for that user), **Add a user** (1.12.0, ADR-083: a name and their access, then Send a link to the email of their Google or Apple account or a pairing code at home; New user at home before), Invite their account (an invitation into that user), the suggestions to bring an account's devices together (whose access stays; the owner's, and only the owner confirms, when the owner's user is one of them), the invitations and, for the owner, the accounts and requests to join, as below, and **Make Dana the owner** on another admin's row (ADR-064, asked first with what changes; `tests/app/owner.test.mjs`). A sixth device is refused with "Remove a device first" and the user's devices (`views/device-limit.js`, also in Settings → Account's Add my other device). Since 1.12.0 every user renames their own devices and themself (admins any), and a device not used for 30 days says "not used since …" (or "never used") next to Remove. `tests/app/users.test.mjs`, `tests/app/add-user.test.mjs`.
- **People and devices** (`#/access` with a controller before 1.9.0, a row on Settings' list, admin keys): every API key with its role (changeable), when it was last used, when it expires ("expires in …", the API console's key, ADR-040), and Revoke; the invitations waiting, with Revoke; for the home's owner, the accounts that belong to the home with their devices, and Remove (their keys are revoked at home first, then the membership ends), and the accounts *Asking to join* with an invitation sent to another email: name, email or *hidden by Apple*, sign-in, the account's age, when it asked, the invitation and the code, with Approve and Refuse (`GET`/`POST /v1/homes/{home}/join-requests`). Devices and invitations come from the controller, people from the account service (`GET /v1/homes/{home}/members`). Each device shows the person (profile) it belongs to; an admin can move it to another person or rename one.
